# Difficulty strategy for ideation, creation, and hardening

Bias new and hardened tasks toward ambitious initial difficulty. It is usually
cheaper and safer to remove, isolate, or relax a challenge layer after trials
than to discover and integrate a strong new layer into an easy task.

- By default, combine multiple evidence-backed failure modes or challenge
  layers, including creatively related or unrelated ones. A single-layer task
  needs a concrete reason why additional strong layers would harm it.
- Preserve the failure-inducing core of every selected layer. Integration must
  not simplify, bypass, disclose, or dilute the reasoning gap that made the
  layer valuable.
- Prefer interactions between layers and end-to-end consequences over a list of
  disconnected chores. Larger scope is desirable when it creates necessary
  cross-component reasoning.
- Overshoot through genuine reasoning, implementation, and debugging demands;
  never through ambiguity, hidden requirements, fragile timing, junk context,
  broken infrastructure, or an unreliable oracle.
- Oracle solvability, spec sufficiency, fairness, verifier feasibility, and
  acceptance of valid alternatives remain hard constraints.
- Treat configured pass-rate targets as the eventual empirical calibration
  goal, not a reason to make the first version conservative. Validate with
  trials, then use `ease-task` when legitimate difficulty overshoots.
